Let's cut through the noise. If you run a business in Eastbourne—whether it's a shop, office, warehouse, or café—you need to know where you stand on electrical compliance. Not because I want to scare you into spending money, but because it's the law, and more importantly, it keeps people safe.

I've been an electrician for over a decade, and I've seen what happens when businesses ignore the basics. Faulty wiring. Equipment that hasn't been tested in years. Emergency lighting that doesn't work when it's actually needed. These aren't small problems; they're risks that can shut down your business, void your insurance, or worse.

The good news? Commercial electrical compliance isn't as complicated as some people make it sound. You just need to know what applies to your business, when it needs doing, and who can do it properly.

The EICR: Your Electrical MOT

An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) is the main inspection you'll need. Think of it as an MOT for your building's electrical system. For most commercial properties, you need an EICR every five years as a baseline.

If you get C1 or C2 codes on your report, you need to act fast. These aren't suggestions—they're identified risks that need fixing before they become bigger problems.

PAT Testing: It's Not Just Kettles

PAT testing covers any electrical equipment that plugs in and can be moved. Computers, power tools, extension leads, and even the office microwave. The HSE is clear: you must ensure equipment is safe, and PAT testing is the straightforward way to prove you're doing that.
